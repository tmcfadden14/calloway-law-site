Signature Matter

The 1890 HBCU Cases

The Second Morrill Act of 1890 established land-grant institutions for Black Americans excluded from the colleges created under the 1862 act — with a federal obligation to fund them commensurately, sustained across generations.

Calloway Law advises and represents interests connected to the accountability of the public bodies charged with meeting those commitments to the nation's nineteen 1890 land-grant HBCUs — matters institutional in scope, concerning the legal obligations of government and the consequences of inequitable federal policy.

Practice 01

Public Interest Litigation

Constitutional and civil rights litigation — with concentrations in civil rights, environmental justice, and consumer protection — brought in state and federal courts, at trial and on appeal.

The firm takes cases whose resolution will matter after the case is over: where the law is unsettled, an institution has failed, or a community has been denied the accountability it is owed. Consequence, not volume, is the measure.

Practice 02

Government Affairs Counsel

Counsel for clients whose interests engage the machinery of government — legislation, regulatory posture, agency engagement, and the conduct of public institutions.

The advice is grounded in direct experience inside government and major American companies: how decisions actually move through agencies, legislatures, and executive offices. Candid assessment and clear strategy, free of bluster.

Calloway Law conducts our non-legal Government Affairs advisory matters through our public affairs consulting firm, Pine Street Advisors.
